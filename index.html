Spain Vacation 2026:
Lake Como-Style Escape

4 scenic options for the relaxed, walkable, no-car-required second half of your Catalonia trip

You've spent 4–5 days trekking the Pyrenees. Now you want the opposite: slow, scenic, and serene. A place where you can walk to the market for fresh bread and cheese, hike from your doorstep, and watch the sunset without a crowd. Like Lake Como, but Catalan. Here are four options that fit the brief.
TL;DR

Trip at a glance: 10 days, August 2026. Fly into/out of Barcelona (BCN). 4–5 days self-planned Pyrenees trek, then 4–6 days in one of these four options: Cadaqués (coastal artist village), Besalú (medieval stone town + volcanic park), Tossa de Mar (walled castle town by the sea), or Rupit (cliffside mountain village, most affordable).

What We're Looking For

A quiet, picturesque town with minimal crowds, walkable to markets and amenities, accessible without a car, offering nearby hikes and relaxed outings — similar to the Lake Como Airbnb experience from four years ago. Budget: under $300/night for a small hotel, B&B, or Airbnb.

Option 1: Cadaqués

🏘️ The Whitewashed Fisherman's Escape · Best for: Coastal beauty, art history, stunning walks, sunset picnics
Cadaqués is a whitewashed fishing town on a wide bay at the tip of the Cap de Creus peninsula. Narrow cobbled streets, artists' studios tucked into corners, and the legacy of Salvador Dalí everywhere. Once isolated behind coastal hills, it still feels like a secret — especially in the early morning and evening when day-trippers thin out. Think Mediterranean fishing village meets bohemian artists' colony.
Why it fits the Lake Como brief: Panoramic bay views from hillside apartments — that "sweeping lake view" but over the Mediterranean. Morning markets and local shops along Carrer Major for fresh bread, cheese, cured meats, and wine. Cap de Creus Natural Park trails start right from town. The coastal path to Portlligat (30-minute walk to Dalí's house) and the dramatic lighthouse trail are stunning. Evenings are serene — the lack of train access keeps crowds lower than the southern Costa Brava.

Option 2: Besalú + La Garrotxa

🏰 Medieval Village & Volcanic Landscapes · Best for: History, authenticity, hiking volcanic craters, budget-friendly
Besalú is a perfectly preserved 12th-century stone town. The iconic Romanesque bridge arches over the Fluvià River, and the old town is completely pedestrian — cobbled lanes, stone homes, remnants of a Jewish quarter with the Iberian Peninsula's only known Jewish purification baths (mikveh). It feels like stepping into medieval Catalonia. The broader Garrotxa region is a volcanic wonderland: dormant craters, beech forests, and natural swimming holes.
Why it fits the Lake Como brief: Entirely car-free old town — purely pedestrian, incredibly peaceful in evenings. Local bakery, butcher, small grocery for market-to-picnic provisioning. The Garrotxa Volcanic Zone Natural Park has well-marked trails through craters and beech forests. Much quieter than coastal towns in August — authentic Catalan life, few international tourists. Budget-friendly: rural farmhouses (masias) and Airbnbs at $100–200/night. You can combine with a Banyoles lake day trip (20 km away).

Option 3: Tossa de Mar

🏰 The Walled Old Town by the Sea · Best for: Castle-town atmosphere, easy access from Barcelona, swimming coves
Tossa de Mar has the most dramatic silhouette on the Costa Brava: a fortified medieval old town (Vila Vella) perched on a rocky headland, crumbling castle walls, and crescent beaches on either side. Forbes named it one of Europe's most beautiful beach destinations. The old town becomes quiet after day-trippers leave, and it's the last remaining walled medieval town on the Catalan coast.
Why it fits the Lake Como brief: The Vila Vella is entirely car-free — castle walls, lookout towers, hidden alleys, panoramic sea views. Saturday market, plus daily bakeries and fishmongers near the port. Coastal path to Cala Giverola (beautiful cove, 45-minute walk) and the lighthouse trail. Direct bus from Barcelona: only 1½ hours, making it the easiest to reach. Much calmer than neighboring Lloret de Mar — stay in or near the old town, not the resort strip.

Option 4: Rupit

⛰️ Dramatic Cliffside Mountain Village · Best for: Maximum tranquility, dramatic scenery, budget-friendly, swimming holes
A thousand-year-old stone village accessed by a swaying wooden suspension bridge over a gorge. Rupit is car-free, tiny, and perched on the edge of the Collsacabra cliffs. Below it, the 115-meter Salt de Sallent (Catalonia's highest waterfall) cascades into a deep ravine. It feels like a mountain village in the Dolomites, but distinctly Catalan — the most "mountain Lake Como" of the four options.
Why it fits the Lake Como brief: 100% car-free — park (or bus drop-off) at the entrance, then cross the hanging bridge on foot. Extremely few international tourists — incredibly quiet even in August. Local bakery, small grocery, family-run restaurants in the village square. The Salt de Sallent waterfall trail starts from town, and the Collsacabra cliff hike offers jaw-dropping views. Swimming holes in the gorge for those hot August afternoons. Rural masias and Airbnbs under $80–150/night — the most affordable option.
Trade-offs: Fewer restaurant choices (maybe 3–4 options). Remote: requires train + bus (~2 hours from BCN). Limited nightlife.

August-Specific Tips

  • Book everything early. August is peak Spanish vacation month — trains, buses, and accommodations fill up fast, especially the best Airbnbs with views.
  • Siesta is real. Shops close roughly 1:30–5:00 PM. Plan market runs for morning.
  • Heat management. Coastal options (Cadaqués, Tossa) benefit from sea breezes. Mountain/inland options (Besalú, Rupit) can be hot midday — plan hikes for early morning or late afternoon.
  • Swimming. Cadaqués and Tossa have great sea swimming. Besalú has river access; Rupit has natural river pools in the gorge.
  • Dalí sites book out. If Cadaqués appeals, reserve the Casa-Museu in Portlligat weeks in advance — it has strict entry limits.
